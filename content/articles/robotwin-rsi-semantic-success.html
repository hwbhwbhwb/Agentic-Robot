1. From Reaching a Target to Completing the Task

RoboTwin 2.0's original success criteria, which we call Origin-SR, preserve compatibility with existing expert programs, and VLA/WAM data. They do not always express the natural-language task itself. Many original check_success() implementations test whether an object center, functional point, joint angle, or height enters a threshold region. They may omit the acting gripper, orientation, contact, release, action order, or stable hold required by the instruction, while adding an expert-specific endpoint that the instruction never mentions.

Semantic has 18 more successful seeds, but the policy did not become stronger. The same frozen rollouts were read by two different success definitions. Some Origin successes are semantic false positives; some Semantic successes expose false negatives caused by expert-specific endpoints.

This distinction matters for zero-shot agents. VLA/WAM data collection and fine-tuning can legitimately treat an expert endpoint as part of the data protocol. A zero-shot agent should not have to reproduce one expert route if another physically valid behavior satisfies the instruction. We therefore report Origin-SR and Semantic-SR side by side: the first preserves comparability, while the second asks whether the language-specified task was actually completed.

2. RSI: Develop the Policy, Then Freeze the Evaluation

RSI places the task instruction, simulation environment, generated controller, and evaluator in one development loop. A coding agent writes executable control code, runs it in simulation, reads the resulting success state or diagnostics, and revises the policy. The loop ends when a policy artifact is selected or the development budget is exhausted.

Development and final evaluation are separate. During development, the agent can inspect feedback and modify code. The selected policy is then frozen, executed on new seeds without further coding-agent intervention, and evaluated with both Origin-SR and Semantic-SR on the same rollout. “RSI found a usable controller” and “the frozen controller completed this seed” are different claims, so accepted development cases cannot be reported as the final success rate.

RoboDojo's offline-policy framing offers a useful parallel: evaluator feedback helps form a policy during development, then the frozen policy runs in batches without the coding agent. RoboTwin and RoboDojo differ in tasks, perception, and control, but both require a clean boundary between policy development and frozen-policy evaluation.

3. Why Zero-Shot Evaluation Needs a Semantic Fix

3.1 The valid scope of the original checker

Many RoboTwin 2.0 tasks were designed for VLA/WAM data collection, fine-tuning, and evaluation. The RoboTwin 2.0 paper presents the project as both a scalable data generator and a benchmark, with an expert-data synthesis pipeline that uses multimodal language models and simulation-in-the-loop refinement to generate task-level execution code. In that setting, predefined expert programs, target poses, and endpoint regions are part of the data protocol. Measuring whether a model reproduces the same endpoint is reasonable, and retaining the native checker keeps new results comparable with prior RoboTwin VLA/WAM evaluation.

The mismatch appears when the same checker is applied directly to a zero-shot agent. Such an agent interprets language and searches for a feasible action sequence instead of imitating fixed expert actions. An implicit waypoint, positional offset, or terminal pose should not become mandatory unless the instruction or task specification requires it.

This concern is also visible in public discussion around the official repository. RoboTwin Issue #475 documents instruction templates that conflict with play_once() in temporal order, concurrency, or arm assignment. It also notes that final-state success checks can miss whether the policy followed the instruction literally. Issue #493 identifies a different failure mode using adjust_bottle: evaluation can stop when a position or height threshold is first crossed, without confirming that the resulting state remains physically valid. Its proposed stable-success mode would continue simulation for a hold window and optionally inspect velocity, gripper state, contact, and workspace bounds. The first issue concerns language-to-trajectory alignment; the second concerns temporal and physical validity. Together they show why a final endpoint alone is insufficient for zero-shot language-following evaluation.

Zero-WAM provides a concrete engineering precedent. For unseen-task generalization, it revises the RoboTwin criteria for move_stapler_pad and stamp_seal. The former requires the released stapler to lie within and physically contact the pad regardless of yaw; the latter expands the target-plane tolerance from 1 cm to 3 cm. Zero-WAM also preserves the upstream task files for standard in-domain RoboTwin evaluation. This does not establish a complete semantic evaluator for all 50 tasks, but it supports the protocol used here: keep Origin-SR for comparability and report a separate language-aligned metric for zero-shot behavior.

3.2 adjust_bottle: position is not the task

The instruction asks the robot to pick up the bottle with the correct arm and keep its head pointing upward. The original checker primarily reads the bottle's functional point:

It does not verify bottle-head orientation, contact by the correct gripper, continued support, or sustained stability. It therefore measures whether the bottle reaches the expert program's elevated lateral region more directly than whether the instruction was completed.

3.3 Two ways to resolve the mismatch

Route A

Adapt the prompt to the checker

Write the checker's endpoint, position, orientation, and action sequence into the instruction. This preserves the original evaluator with little implementation change, but risks turning a natural-language task into a checker specification.

Route B

Align the checker with the prompt

Keep the task language and evaluate physical ground truth, object relationships, contact, and the required trajectory history. This is the route used for Semantic-SR, while Origin-SR remains available for comparison.

Semantic-SR is a complementary metric, not a renamed official RoboTwin success rate. Origin-SR preserves historical and data-protocol comparability; Semantic-SR evaluates whether a zero-shot policy completed the language task through a reasonable physical process.

4. What Semantic Success Checks

A typical Origin predicate asks whether a point entered a position or height window, sometimes combined with a gripper or joint condition. Semantic decomposes the instruction into the subset of history, role, geometry, physics, end-state, temporal, and safety predicates that the task actually needs.

HHistoryOrder of grasp, lift, release, handover, press, pour, or shake events.
RRoleObject identity, correct arm-to-object mapping, and non-duplicated objects.
GGeometryObject-local geometry, AABB or footprint, relative pose, coverage, and clearance.
PPhysicsActual contact, load bearing, table clearance, and support relationships.
TTemporalSustained conditions, velocity limits, synchronization, and pose drift.
SSafetyWorkspace bounds, upright containers, and no obvious deep interpenetration.

Different tasks use different subsets. For adjust_bottle, the correct gripper must contact and lift the bottle, keep its head within roughly 20° of vertical, and maintain those conditions for about 0.5 seconds with bounded pose drift. For pick_dual_bottles, each arm must grasp the correct bottle, both bottles must leave the table within the allowed time window, and the dual hold must remain stable. For shake_bottle_horizontally, commanded oscillations do not count unless the bottle's long axis completes real horizontal crossings.

Diagnostic fields explain why a predicate failed, but only predicates used in the final Boolean return define success. A log field named contact or lifted is not independently sufficient.

5. Experimental Setup and Results Across 50 Tasks

The experiment covers all 50 official RoboTwin tasks and uses the first 20 seeds from the official 50-seed list, giving 20 rollouts per task and 1,000 rollouts overall. For each task, one policy artifact selected after RSI development was frozen before evaluation, and Origin and Semantic evaluated the same rollout.

Semantic reported 182 successful rollouts, Origin reported 164, and both were true on the same seed 124 times. The 18-rollout difference measures evaluator disagreement, not a policy improvement.

Δ is Semantic successes minus Origin successes for the same task. It describes evaluator output, not a gain in policy performance.

  • Semantic is higher when Origin binds success to the wrong endpoint. Semantic exceeds Origin most clearly on grab_roller (17 vs. 1), move_can_pot (10 vs. 2), adjust_bottle (8 vs. 1), and pick_dual_bottles (5 vs. 0). In these tasks, the policy produced a physical state consistent with the instruction but did not reach the expert-specific endpoint, or Origin selected an unsuitable observable. Semantic measures table clearance, correct contact, synchronization, and stable support instead of simply lowering a threshold.
  • Origin is higher when a transient state is mistaken for a complete behavior. Origin exceeds Semantic on lift_pot (10 vs. 3), shake_bottle_horizontally (10 vs. 0), open_laptop (7 vs. 3), shake_bottle (8 vs. 4), and place_object_scale (4 vs. 2). A height event, hinge ratio, or approximate geometric state can trigger Origin before contact attribution, action order, release, or stable hold is established.
  • When both scores are low, the policy is the first place to look. stack_blocks_two, blocks_ranking_rgb, blocks_ranking_size, and several placement or stacking tasks remain near zero under both definitions. Their failures are more consistent with incomplete action chains: monocular reconstruction breaks after grasping, dual-arm actions become serialized, release and retreat checks fail, or the policy enters safe_hold without regrasping or replanning. Semantic-SR helps separate evaluator disagreement from genuine policy failure.

6. Case Studies

adjust_bottle: a Semantic-only success

Origin succeeds on 1/20 seeds and Semantic on 8/20, with no seed accepted by both. On seed 000, the bottle reaches approximately 1.0055 m, its upright angle is approximately 5.08°, the correct gripper remains in contact, and the stable window reaches 129/125 steps. Its x position is approximately 0.007, so it never enters Origin's lateral target region. Seed 014 shows the opposite mismatch: x=0.1519 m and height=0.9143 m satisfy Origin, but the upright angle is 91.49°, leaving the bottle nearly horizontal. All 12 Semantic failures end with upright=false, which identifies righting after lift as the main policy bottleneck.

grab_roller: measuring the physical state the instruction names

Origin succeeds on 1/20 seeds and Semantic on 17/20. Origin checks whether both gripper commands are closed and whether the roller center exceeds 0.8 m. Semantic instead verifies actual contact with both handles, at least about 2.5 cm of bottom clearance, and roughly 75 stable physics steps. On seed 000, the roller bottom is about 3.47 cm above the table and the dual grasp remains stable for 90/75 steps, even though the center does not cross Origin's threshold.

pick_dual_bottles: synchronization matters more than final height

Origin succeeds on 0/20 seeds and Semantic on 5/20. The successful Semantic seeds assign each bottle to the correct arm, lift both from the table within the allowed timing window, and maintain a stable overlapping hold. Their first-lift differences are 1 or 7 physics steps, with final stable windows of 100 to 121 steps. Other trajectories lift both bottles 39 to 49 steps apart, so the final image may look close while the synchronized dual-grasp requirement remains unsatisfied.

shake_bottle_horizontally: a height event is not a shake

Origin succeeds on 10/20 seeds while Semantic succeeds on 0/20. Origin records whether the bottle center ever reaches a height threshold. Semantic requires a valid grasp, table clearance, stable lift, at least two horizontal crossings of the bottle's long axis with hysteresis, and a safe final hold. On most Origin-success seeds, horizontal_crossings=0. The generated policy counts commanded oscillations through shake_index, but it does not read actual crossings, contact, or stability before deciding whether the behavior is complete.

stack_blocks_two: evaluator changes cannot repair a missing action chain

Both Origin and Semantic score 0/20. On 14 seeds, held-object multi-view reconstruction stalls after the first grasp. The remaining trajectories fail during release, retreat, or low-altitude transport of the second block. Seed 000 is the only rollout to reach second-block transfer, but it still has about 6.4 cm of lateral error. The green block remains in the gripper, with no release, support relationship, or stable stack. This is a perception, planning, and recovery failure, not a reason to relax the evaluator.

7. Implications for RSI and Benchmark Design

  • Monocular geometry is a recurring single point of failure. After grasping, gripper occlusion makes object edges, axes, dimensions, and relative pose difficult to reconstruct reliably.
  • Synchronized dual-arm tasks cannot be reduced to serial checks. Lifting one object and then the other may produce a plausible final frame while missing the required timing window.
  • RGB motion is not a contact-control loop. An object rising in the image does not prove correct fingertip contact, table clearance, or freedom from gripper slip.
  • Failure recovery needs state-aware replanning. Closing the gripper and entering safe_hold cannot recover bottle righting, resynchronize a dual lift, or finish a stack.
  • One task specification should generate the prompt, expert trajectory, and evaluator. This avoids describing one goal, demonstrating another route, and measuring a third endpoint.

Future reports should publish Origin, Semantic, Both, Origin-only, and Semantic-only together, then connect representative seeds to terminal JSON, predicate diagnostics, and video. Semantic-SR does not complete the control task for the policy. It tells us whether the next repair belongs in perception, planning, contact control, timing, or recovery.

8. Conclusion

This 50-task experiment should not be compressed into one “best success rate.” Origin-SR preserves historical comparability. Semantic-SR asks whether a zero-shot policy actually completed the instruction. Their disagreement is part of the result because it reveals where benchmark endpoints, language, and physical behavior diverge.

This Semantic Fix has been open-sourced, but gaps and omissions are inevitable. We welcome the community to help identify shortcomings, refine the predicates, and improve the evaluation together.
